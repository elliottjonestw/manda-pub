How good is Manda's image OCR?

The Manda Team · September 20, 2026 · 5 min read

How well can Manda read Chinese from an image? We tried signs, packaging, a notice, a menu, a book page, and a historical newspaper. Manda turns a photo or scan into editable text through Import Text → Upload → Upload Image. The examples below show what it extracted and where you may need to check the image yourself.

We uploaded the 10 images below through Upload Image. They are real photographs or scans found online, not AI-generated images. Most come from Wikimedia Commons; the book page comes from a photograph published by Epigram Bookshop. The images shown are the exact files we uploaded. Most were cropped to focus on the relevant text; the candy package and book page were also rotated to make the text upright. We did not retouch or replace any letters. The cover illustration is separate from the test set.

The blocks headed Manda's result reproduce the Upload Image output verbatim, including errors, extra symbols, and mixed character forms. These are qualitative examples, not an estimate of accuracy across all photos. No words were hand-corrected in the OCR results.

1. A clear two-character sign

Easy: large Traditional Chinese characters on a plain background.

Image: Chinese squat toilet sign by Hippietrail, CC BY-SA 4.0. Cropped from the source photograph.

Manda's result:

蹲式

Assessment: Exact: 蹲式 is the entire Chinese sign.

What this tells us

Manda reads short signs accurately in these examples and recovers most of the notice, food label, road sign, and candy packaging. The book page becomes readable, editable prose with a few character errors. The restaurant menu yields many dish names, but its prices and row structure need closer checking.

Small type and crowded layouts can produce wrong characters, misplaced prices, and scrambled descriptions. The old newspaper is a failure case. Compare extracted text with the image before relying on it, especially for prices, directions, or study material. You can correct the text in the editor before opening it in the reader.

To try your own image, open Import Text, choose Upload → Upload Image, and select a photo or scan. A close, sharp, evenly lit crop helps. OCR runs in your browser; the first use loads the recognition models and may take longer.
